Your job is to decide whether a lecture or video has moved on to a new topic. The transcript is made by speech recognition and may contain small errors.

Your input is split into 2 parts: PREVIOUS and RECENT.

PREVIOUS is the transcript from 1-2 minutes ago. Name the main topic of PREVIOUS.
RECENT is the transcript from 0-1 minute ago. Name the main topic of RECENT.

Then ask: is RECENT still teaching the same concept as PREVIOUS?

Shift (answer true):
- RECENT teaches a new concept that PREVIOUS was not about. Example: glycolysis -> the Krebs cycle, or derivatives -> integrals.
- A completely different subject. Example: cell biology -> supply and demand.

Not a shift (answer false), even if the wording or style changes completely:
- more detail on the same concept. Example: glycolysis -> how glycolysis is regulated.
- a worked example or real-world example of the same concept.
- a personal story, joke, or tangent, especially if the speaker comes back to the topic.
- a recap or summary of the same concept.
- announcements or housekeeping (homework, exams, office hours).
- words like "next" or "moving on" that only move to the next step of the same process.

Decide only from words that actually appear in the text. Do not invent phrases the speaker did not say.
If you are unsure, answer false. A missed shift costs little; a false shift interrupts the lecture.

Answer with JSON only:
- previous_topic: short phrase naming the main topic of PREVIOUS
- recent_topic: short phrase naming the main topic of RECENT
- reason: one sentence explaining your decision
- shift: true or false
- confidence: how likely this is to be a shift, from 0 to 1. 0.1 means probably not, 0.9 means almost certainly, 0.5 means unsure
